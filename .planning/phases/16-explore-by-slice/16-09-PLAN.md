# Phase 16-09 — The reserved rooms, and the ev-accounts handoff

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development
> (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps
> use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Every room that has no source says what it is for and asks for nothing — and the
asks that would fill those rooms go to the repo that can serve them, written once, with the
evidence attached.

**Architecture:** Two halves that belong together because each is the other's justification.
The **rooms** are the UI consequence of "a module with no real source ships as a reserved
empty room, never as invented civic record". The **handoff** is the document that turns each
of those rooms into a request someone can act on. Writing them apart produces either rooms
nobody will ever fill or a wish-list with nothing pointing at it.

**Nine** reserved rooms get their final copy here, in three groups: **three** with no source
anywhere in the stack (ballot measures; a federal bill summary to pair with the tally; a
debate schedule), **one** with a partial source we are choosing not to build on
(per-legislator votes), and **five** slots for the coming Connect features (symposiums,
civic listening, common grounds, focused communities, slice action votes). 3 + 1 + 5 = 9.

Four more are **empty states of real modules** — `agenda` (16-06), `races` and `debates`
(16-07) and `coming-up` (16-02). Those were written in their own plans; this plan only
re-reads them so all thirteen speak with one voice.

Five of the nine become asks in the handoff. The other four are DESIGN non-goals and ask for
nothing: the slot is the deliverable.

**Tech Stack:** React 19 · TypeScript 5.7 · Tailwind v4 · Markdown · no test framework (see `16-SHARED.md`).

**Spec:** `16-DESIGN.md` ("What actually serves decision 10" — the table is the handoff's
spine; "One route, not three"; the non-goals list), `16-CONTEXT.md` (the four corrections;
"This phase is frontend-only… the route and record shape go out as a handoff doc, following
the `TT-HANDOFF.md` / `ACCOUNTS-HANDOFF.md` precedent from Phase 15"),
`16-RESEARCH.md` (Contradictions 1, 2, 4; Findings 1, 2 and 6; the Appendix of exact calls).

**Precedent to read before Task 3:** `.planning/phases/15-tool-deep-links/TT-HANDOFF.md` and
`ACCOUNTS-HANDOFF.md`. Both are real, both got real replies, and the shape is the reason —
a numbered ask, the evidence behind it, and what this repo does once it lands.

**Depends on:** 16-08. **Execute serially after it** — see the plan-set table in 16-01.
Task 2 **extends** `scripts/check-explore-rooms.mjs`, which 16-08 creates. Logically the

### Task 1: Write nine rooms that are a welcome, not an apology

**Files:**
- Modify: `src/components/explore/registry.tsx`
- Reference (do not edit): `src/components/explore/modules/SeedInvitationModule.tsx` (16-08)
  — the voice to match; `exploreUi.tsx` — the vocabulary

- [ ] **Step 1: Pick the voice, once**

Every room is two short sentences: **what this space is for**, and **what has to exist for
it to fill**. Present tense for the first, plain for the second. No dates, no "soon", no
roadmap, no apology.

Worked examples of the difference, for the measures room:

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| "Ballot measures coming soon!" | The dead end goal 6 forbids. |
| "We're sorry, ballot measure data is not available for your area." | Apologetic, and *"for your area"* is false — it is not available for **anywhere**. |
| "Proposition 12: Clean Water Act — Sample" | 🔴 Invented civic record. Never. |
| "When the measures on your ballot are published, they will appear here with what a yes and a no would each do." | What it is for, and what has to exist. |

Two rules that catch most mistakes:

- 🔴 **Never say "for your area" or "in your community" when the thing is unavailable
  everywhere.** It tells a member their place is the poorly-served one, which is both untrue
  and dispiriting. Ballot measures, bill summaries and debate schedules are missing for
  everyone. Agenda coverage genuinely *is* per-place — that room may say so.
- Say what a member would get, not what we would build. "What a yes and a no would each do"
  describes a benefit; "measure ingestion" describes a ticket.

- [ ] **Step 2: Write all eleven**

Work down the table above. Keep each to two sentences and a single clause of detail. Read the
five Connect rooms (`symposiums`, `civic-listening`, `common-grounds`, `focused-communities`,
`slice-action-votes`) consecutively and out loud — they will sit near each other on the page,
and eleven variations of the same sentence is its own kind of noise. Vary the shape, keep the
voice.

`slice-action-votes` earns one extra line: it is the same mechanic as the seed vote, at the
other end of a topic's life. Saying so is honest and sets the expectation that when voting
arrives it arrives in both places.

- [ ] **Step 3: Count them on a page**

A city composition now lists `ballot-measures`, `seed-invitation` and five Connect rooms
after its real content. Load it and look: if the page reads as mostly promises, **cut the
number of rooms shown at once** rather than shortening the copy. Options, in order of
preference:

1. Group the five Connect rooms into one room that names them — one card, five sentences.
   Reserving the slots in the registry is what the design asks for; rendering five separate
   cards is not required by it.
2. Render the furthest-off rooms only below a "What's coming to this space" divider.

The design's requirement is that each feature has somewhere obvious to arrive without
re-opening the design. A `ModuleId` and a registry entry satisfy that whether or not it gets
its own card today. The anti-goal — **overwhelming the member** — does not bend.

Whichever you choose, keep all eleven `ModuleId`s and all eleven entries. The grouping is a
rendering decision, not a registry one.

- [ ] **Step 4: Typecheck and commit**

```bash
npm run build
git add src/components/explore/registry.tsx
git commit -m "feat(explore): reserved rooms that say what they are for"
```

### Task 3: Write `EXPLORE-HANDOFF.md`

The phase is frontend-only, and this is the artefact that makes that a decision rather than a
limitation. Phase 15 set the precedent twice and both documents got substantive replies —
because each ask carried the evidence behind it and said what the consumer would do with it.

**Files:**
- Create: `.planning/phases/16-explore-by-slice/EXPLORE-HANDOFF.md`
- Reference: `.planning/phases/15-tool-deep-links/TT-HANDOFF.md` (the shape — read the
  opening and `Ask 1` before writing), `ACCOUNTS-HANDOFF.md` (the register, for a handoff
  that is partly questions rather than requests)

- [ ] **Step 1: The frame**

```markdown
# ev-accounts — request from Civic Spaces: one Explore route, four gaps, and a bug

**From:** Civic Spaces (`C:\Civic Spaces`), Phase 16
**Written:** 2026-10-XX
**Audience:** whoever works `C:\EV-Accounts` next
**Companion docs:** `16-DESIGN.md` (the product decisions) and `16-RESEARCH.md` (every call
behind the claims below, with responses) in this directory.
```

Then "Why Civic Spaces is asking", in one short section: each member is placed into civic
slices and each slice's Explore is now bespoke to its jurisdiction, keyed `(slice_type,
geoid)`. The content a slice needs comes from what that place's government is working on. We
built everything reachable from the public API; these are the pieces that are not.

🔴 **State up front what is already working, and name it.** `GET /api/meetings/:id/agenda-items`
carries exactly what this design needed — verbatim titles, legislation references, stages,
plain-language summaries and source links — and it made Bloomington's Explore bespoke with
nobody at Empowered Vote writing a word about Bloomington. The debate and forum recordings
joining to races by `raceIds` were a source our design had written off as non-existent. A
handoff that opens with four complaints gets read as a complaint.

- [ ] **Step 2: Ask 1 — one route for the composition**

`GET /api/civic-spaces/explore?sliceType=&geoid=`, returning the whole composition: the
ordered module ids, the topics with their level, geoid and provenance, and the bespoke layer
already resolved server-side.

Include:

- The **full record shape**, copied from `src/types/explore.ts` — that file is the contract
  and it is already written and in use.
- Why one route and not the three the mock stands in for (`explore/topics`,
  `explore/suggestions`, `updates`): the composition itself now varies by slice, so a single
  response is the natural signature. "What's new" is product release notes, not slice
  content, and stays separate.
- That `civic_spaces/` in ev-accounts currently contains only the assignment engine, and
  `/api/civic-spaces/explore` 404s today (verified live, along with `/explore/topics` and
  `/updates`).
- 🔴 The **provenance** field, and why it is not optional: three content sources have to
  coexist on one page — staff, a local chapter such as the League of Women Voters, and a
  government body's own agenda — and the UI has to be able to say honestly which is which.
  Add the specific sub-ask that a machine-written summary is **flagged as one**. We infer it
  today from a migration comment; a field would be better than an inference.
- What Civic Spaces does once it lands: `src/lib/exploreData.ts` is the seam and the swap is
  that one `queryFn`; `src/lib/agendaTopics.ts` and `src/hooks/useAgendaItems.ts` are then
  deleted, and the two client-side requests collapse into one.

- [ ] **Step 3: Asks 2–5, each with its evidence**

Same shape every time: what we want, what we checked, what we would do with it.

**Ask 2 — ballot measures.** Not served anywhere. Evidence: no `measures` key on any of 8
live responses; the schema is exactly `elections`, `races`, `race_candidates` (migration
`042_election_schema.sql`); a grep of all migrations and `backend/src` for
ballot/measure/referendum/proposition names returns nothing; **zero** measure-like names
among 548 distinct `position_name`s. Note `GET /api/essentials/voter-info` is address-keyed
(Google Civic) and returns polling places and sample-ballot URLs only — unusable here,
because this app never takes an address. Say plainly that we are **not asking for a
timeline**, only whether it is on anyone's map, because the room stays reserved either way.

**Ask 3 — a plain-language bill summary.** The highest-value single ask in the document, and
worth saying so. Decision 8 put a federal vote first in the design — *"recent federal votes
with a plain-language summary of the bill and then the tally"* — and the tally is there while
the summary is not: `summary` empty on every one of ~350 sampled bill rows, `topic_tags`
null, and federal vote rows carrying no bill title at all, only the roll-call question
string (`On Cloture on the Motion to Proceed H.R. 7147 RC# 39`). A tally without a summary is
a number nobody can act on, so the module ships as a reserved room. Even a bill **title** on
the federal vote row would change what we can build.

**Ask 4 — upcoming debates.** The recordings are excellent and already used. What does not
exist is anything scheduled: every `debate` and `forum` row is published, so
*"watch the debate for your race"* shipped and *"the debate for your race is on Thursday"*
cannot. Note the LWV candidate forum in Bloomington's data as evidence that the partner
model the design wants is already present.

**Ask 5 — slice-scoped legislative activity.** There is no route for "what is this
legislature working on" — no bill list, no search, no calendar; the only legislative data is
per politician id, and a slice-level view would be N calls over the member's representatives.
Add the coverage numbers (5 of 12 probed members had votes; federal stale to 2026-03-02 while
the CA Assembly is current to 2026-09-01) and ask whether the staleness is ingestion lag or
by design — our research flagged that as an assumption, not a finding.

- [ ] **Step 4: 🔴 Ask 6 — the elections state-area behaviour. This one is a bug report.**

Separate it visually from the feature asks, because it is a different kind of thing and it
affects consumers who have never heard of Civic Spaces.

`POST /api/essentials/browse/elections-by-area`, verified live 2026-10-24:

| Query | Result | Why it is wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Indiana state area `18/G4000` | all **9** US House districts | a member has one |
| Bloomington city `1805860/G4110` | US House District 9 only | correct |
| North Carolina state area `37/G4000` | **no** `U.S. Senate North Carolina` | Asheville's **city** area has it |
| California state area `06/G4000` | 493 races, **no Governor**, 456 KB | LA's **city** area has the Governor |
| `US/G4000` | **HTTP 500** twice, then a timeout: `{"code":"INTERNAL_ERROR","message":"Failed to fetch election data"}` | a federal lookup is not unreasonable to attempt |

So a state-area query is wrong in both directions at once: it returns races that are not the
member's **and** omits the statewide races that are. Include the probable cause as a lead
rather than a claim — `stateAbbrev` appears to be resolved from a `geofence_boundaries` row
for the queried area, and statewide races are matched only when it is non-null
(`getOverlappingGeoIdsForArea`, `getElectionsByGeoIds`) — and label it an inference, because
the behaviour is verified and the cause is not.

🔴 Flag the blast radius: **Treasury Tracker consumes the same endpoint family.** Any consumer
asking a state-level question about races is getting the same wrong answer, and unlike us
they may not have noticed. Civic Spaces has already worked around it (`racesAreaFor` queries
the most local area), so nothing here is blocked — we are reporting it because it is not
only ours.

- **"What Civic Spaces does once each of these lands"** — one line per ask. Keeps the

- **"Acceptance, from the consumer side"** — for Ask 1 specifically, what a response has to

Also record the one **coverage** note that is not an ask: the agenda pipeline runs in one

## Out of Scope

- **Building any of the five Connect features.** Symposiums, Civic Listening, Common
  Grounds, Focused Communities and slice action votes are DESIGN non-goals. *"This design
  reserves their slots and stops."* The slot is the deliverable.
- **Building `legislator-votes`.** Reachable and partial. Research Open Question 5's default
  is reserve, do not build, and the planner has no mandate to widen scope. If the owner wants
  it, it is a small plan on top of this one — the room and the registry entry already exist.
- **Anything in the ev-accounts repo.** This is a request, not a change. Nothing in Phase 16
  waits on it, which is the point of the frontend-only decision.
- **Chasing the elections state-area bug into ev-accounts.** We report it with evidence and a
  probable cause. Fixing it there is their call, and Civic Spaces is already correct without
  the fix.
- **Asking for agenda coverage in a particular city.** The pipeline runs where it runs.
- Adding a frontend test framework.
